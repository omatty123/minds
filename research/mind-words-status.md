# MIND pronunciation collection — 10 October 2026

User requirement: 50 languages, **recorded human voices only**. Synthesized speech is explicitly excluded.

## Current source selection

50 distinct languages have a word source, a corresponding human recording and an identified reuse license in `mind-words.json`. The collection has 50 human recordings. Romanian and Hausa preserve short phrases containing the selected word, rather than isolated-word recordings. Both are labeled in the list and player. Regional accents are not counted as separate languages. Norwegian is identified as Bokmål for its written form; the recording is catalogued as Norwegian. Cantonese and Mandarin have separate recordings. Esperanto is identified as a constructed language with a human speaker.

The research pass considered 146 distinct language–word pairs in English Wiktionary, then selected matching recordings from Wikimedia Commons / Lingua Libre. Many entries have no audio, an ambiguous homograph, a different language's recording, or an insufficient lexical definition. This was a targeted search, not an exhaustive inventory of every pronunciation repository. Raw API snapshots are kept locally in the ignored `.impeccable/research/mind-words/` directory.

Short English glosses are contextual, not complete cultural definitions. The French and Swedish recordings include an article, explicitly noted in the player. Hebrew and Greek entries distinguish mind from the anatomical brain. The Russian file credits the Shtooka Project without identifying an individual speaker. All recordings preserve source pronunciation; no synthetic or voice-cloned replacements are used.

## Context and future review

Romanian `minte` is heard in `ține minte` (“remember,” literally “keep mind”); the phrase has its own linked dictionary entry. Hausa `hankali` is heard in `rashin hankali`; the noun is documented by HausaDictionary.com, and the source recording is categorized as Hausa and used on Hausa Wiktionary's `Rashin_Hankali` entry. Neither phrase is cut to fabricate a standalone recording. French and Swedish include an article.

Persian `ravān` is labeled psyche/soul/spirit, with a note distinguishing `zehn`. Occitan `esperit` links both its word entry and the English mind translation table; mind/spirit/soul are contextual senses. Do not collapse these into one universal definition.

Future review should prioritize native-speaker checks, standalone recordings for the two phrase entries, and additional coverage such as Hindi, Thai, Vietnamese, Māori, Finnish and Yoruba. Te Aka Māori Dictionary supplies a lexical source for hinengaro: https://maoridictionary.co.nz/search?keywords=hinengaro. Definition access does not establish permission to redistribute its recording.

## Rejected matches

- `Minda.wav` is described as Kotava, not Malay. It is excluded.
- German `minte`, `sind`, and `sinne` recordings do not supply Romanian, Danish or Swedish pronunciations.
- Polish recordings of `mieli` do not supply Finnish.
- Marathi recordings of मन do not supply Hindi or Nepali.
- Bengali recordings of মন do not supply Assamese.
- The Arabic recording of the verb عَقَلَ does not supply the noun عَقْل. The selected noun recording is separately identified.
- Tashelhit `akal` means earth/land, and is not an alternative recording of Indonesian `akal`.
- Songs and nonmatching homographs were excluded. Two clearly labeled short phrases preserve the selected word in context.

The 50-language target is met with the two phrase-context qualifications above. Further additions should improve linguistic coverage and source review, while preserving the human-recording requirement.
