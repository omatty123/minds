# Body-location explorer — 10 October 2026

Scope: eight selected accounts, separated into research, language/gestures and historical beliefs. This is not an inventory of world traditions or an anatomical claim. `body-locations.json` is the content source; `body_map.py` renders accessible static evidence panels. Coordinates are schematic and marker sizes do not encode frequency.

Anglin (2014), DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2014.08.014: full text checked. The explorer uses the free-response categories, not the later forced-choice diagram results. Recruitment was 206 Rutgers students; response totals vary by item. The self and soul entries show selected categories rather than complete distributions. Full-text copy: https://www.newdualism.org/papers-Jul2020/Anglin-CaC2014.pdf.

Alsmith & Longo (2014): abstract and author-hosted paper checked. The task measures self-location through pointer adjustment or alignment judgments; it does not document spontaneous gestures for “mind.” https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24457520/.

Korean 마음: the gesture toward the heart is Matty Wegehaupt’s contribution on 10 October 2026. The linked lexical source supports word meanings, not prevalence of the gesture. No independent population-level gesture study is claimed.

ASL MIND: HandSpeak documents the sign at the upper side of the head. Its linguistic location does not establish a signer’s philosophy. Historical Egyptian and Chinese examples reuse the existing museum and lexical records in the learning bank; they do not establish ancient pointing practices.
