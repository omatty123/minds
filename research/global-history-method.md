# Global history of MIND — research method and coverage

Edition: 10 October 2026. This is a selected, expandable synthesis, not a complete census of cultures or a systematic review. The requested ambition is global and across time; the current edition has 44 documented accounts. The public coverage section names substantial remaining gaps.

## Source inventory

The complete edition inventory is `history.json`, published unchanged as `dist/history.json`. Every account includes period, region, tradition, disciplines, comparison themes, a short account, a deeper interpretation, a work or encounter, limits, source links, review depth and editorial check date. These 44 records are separate from the existing 53-record learning bank. This distinction keeps the counts honest and preserves the bank's existing schema and syllabus relationship.

Research used primary-text translations, museum collection records, institutional descriptions of living models, Indigenous testimony, specialist philosophical encyclopedias, scholarly publication records, and original research abstracts. It also reused explicitly labeled existing learning-bank records for Jikji, Plato, Dickinson, Sōseki, Clark, Levin, Solms and Berg. Retrieval was uneven: Nature, Chinese Text Project and a paper mirror intermittently failed direct opening; indexed passages or the earlier bank record are labeled accordingly. No claim of a full-paper or full-book review is made when only a passage, synopsis or abstract was checked.

Source-level interpretations appear in the account records. The overview and nine comparison themes are the project's editorial synthesis. No invented quotations or invented historical voices are used. English translations are entry points, not exact equivalences among mind, soul, self, life, awareness and personhood.

## Chronology and comparison

Six broad period groups organize navigation. Individual dates identify works, objects or the documented account. Groups are not stages of civilizational progress, and order within them is approximate rather than a precise time scale. Ancient traditions continue into the present. Modern Māori, Akan and Diné accounts are placed with their modern documentation, not projected into an undated prehistoric past. Archaeological evidence supports inferences about capacities and practices rather than a precise recovered theory of mind.

Region filters are deliberately broad navigation labels; they do not claim cultural homogeneity. The Africa & Europe and Multiple regions labels retain cross-regional work. Religious, philosophical and disciplinary labels overlap. Art and literature annotations are marked as interpretation, not experimental confirmation. Contemporary research retains the distinction between adaptive behavior, intelligence, self-report and conscious experience.

## Coverage still needed

Australian Aboriginal traditions; further African, Pacific and Indigenous American accounts; Southeast and Central Asia; Tibetan traditions; Zoroastrian and Bahá’í sources; further Jewish, Christian, Hindu and Islamic schools; women’s intellectual histories; disability and neurodiversity; psychiatry, linguistics, dance and sport. Expand with particular authors/communities, original-language terms and well-supported works rather than a single generic “Indigenous” or “Eastern” entry. Community or specialist review remains especially valuable for living traditions and translation.
